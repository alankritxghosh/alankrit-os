Last time I went through the research on generalists. The short version: breadth predicts who starts something. It doesn't reliably predict who does well.

That left a question. What changes when you have agents?

**The test that fits best**

Researchers at Stanford and Harvard Business School ran an experiment at IG Group, a UK fintech. [VERIFY: authors and paper title]

78 employees wrote web articles with AI help. [VERIFY]

Three groups.
Experts, who write those articles for a living.
Marketing staff from the same department, who had never written articles but knew roughly what the writers did.
Developers and data scientists with no writing or marketing background. [VERIFY]

The marketing group got close to the experts. The developers didn't. They trailed the marketing and web colleagues by 13% on clarity and competence. [VERIFY: exact measure and figure]

The researchers' label for it is expertise distance. How far you are from the task decides how much AI helps. [VERIFY wording]

**Where this meets the generalist question**

A generalist is, by definition, close to a lot of tasks. Not expert in them. Close.

That is the only version of the claim I can support. Being near a task is what lets you judge what the model gives you back.

**Where it breaks**

BCG ran a study with 758 consultants using GPT-4. [VERIFY]

On tasks inside what the model could do, consultants with AI finished 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, at more than 40% higher quality. [VERIFY]

On a task chosen to sit outside what the model could do, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to get the right answer than those without it. [VERIFY]

That is the risk with breadth. You are close enough to feel confident and not close enough to catch the mistake.

**One more result**

Procter & Gamble ran a field experiment with 776 professionals. [VERIFY] Individuals with AI performed about like teams of two without it. [VERIFY wording]

R&D and Commercial staff, who normally lean technical or commercial, produced more balanced ideas with AI. [VERIFY]

AI seems to lower the cost of working in the lane next to yours.

That is a cost of entry, though. It is a different thing from winning.

**So, sometimes**

Agents seem to widen the circle of tasks you can do well. The circle has an edge, and the edge is where your judgment stops.

A specialist without agents is slow but knows where the edge is.
A generalist with agents is fast but can walk off it without noticing.
