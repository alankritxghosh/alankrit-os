## The problem with the title

The evidence does not say "generalist beats specialist". It says AI helps most when the person is close enough to the task to judge the output. So the honest piece is the title as a real question, answered with "sometimes, and here is the condition". Alankrit decides whether to keep the stronger claim; if so he needs his own evidence for it.

## Evidence (all UNVERIFIED, excerpts only)

1. **IG Group experiment** (Stanford and Harvard Business School DDD Institute, 78 employees, UK fintech). Three groups wrote web articles with AI: expert writers, adjacent outsiders (marketing staff, no article writing experience), distant outsiders (developers and data scientists). Reported: adjacent group nearly matched experts with AI, distant group improved little and lagged marketing and web colleagues by 13% on clarity and competence. Takeaway as reported: the gain depends on "expertise distance". Seen via an HBS library summary and a Harvard Business Review piece ("Gen AI Won't Make Your Employees Experts", 2026-03). Need: authors, paper title, exact figures.
2. **The Cybernetic Teammate** (Dell'Acqua et al., Procter & Gamble, 776 professionals, May to July 2024, GPT-4, preregistered, four conditions: solo, pair, solo+AI, pair+AI). Reported: individuals with AI matched teams without AI. R&D and Commercial staff produced more balanced solutions with AI, bimodality coefficient 0.564 without AI vs 0.482 with. NBER w33641, now in Organization Science. Need: verify the figures and the "matched teams" wording.
3. **Jagged Frontier** (BCG, 758 consultants, GPT-4). Inside the AI frontier: 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, over 40% higher quality. Outside it: consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to be correct. Need: verify, it is the key counterweight.
4. Blog level claims, weak, do not cite as research: "comb-shaped professional", "half-life of a technical skill 2.5 years", "39% of core skills change by 2030".
