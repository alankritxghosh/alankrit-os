## Read this first: limits of this report

- **Small samples.** 4-9 replies per X account and 7-10 comments per LinkedIn account, all from the last ~2 days (2026-10-02/03). One moment in time.
- **Growth is not verified.** I did not check how any of these accounts grew (paid promotion, pods, a viral post, an existing audience). "Organic" is not yet established for any of them.
- **Views are not comparable.** A two-word reply from an account with 200K followers got ~1K views. That reflects the audience, not the reply. It says nothing about what works at 31 followers.
- **The list is large-account heavy.** X: 42K to 967K followers. LinkedIn: 19K to 883K. And the LinkedIn five are GTM and personal-brand coaches, because they come from the playbook in the Personal Brand vault (author UNKNOWN), not from Alankrit's curious-builder lane. Proposed swaps are at the bottom.
- **Most visible "replies" are replies inside their own threads** (to people who commented on them). That tells us about reply-back habits more than about commenting on other people's posts.

## Patterns across all ten

1. **Reply-back.** Every account answers the people who comment on them, usually by first name. At 31 followers Alankrit has few commenters, so the actionable version is: reply to every single comment on his own posts, fast.
2. **Short.** Typical replies are 1-15 words; the longest are 2-3 sentences. One idea each.
3. **Tone matches the parent.** Celebration gets emphasis, a question gets a direct answer, disagreement gets a calm clarifying question.
4. **They celebrate other builders** and often ask a small, specific follow-up.
5. **Casual register on X** (lowercase, banter), slightly warmer and more structured on LinkedIn.
6. **Their own work is what scales.** The biggest numbers seen came from posts showing something they made, not from replies.

How this squares with Alankrit's rules: short, human replies with real content already match his preference. The coaches' emoji-and-exclamation style does not match his voice rules, and the generic AI-sounding comments seen under Lieben's post are the opposite of what he asked for.
